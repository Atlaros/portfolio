title: TramitIA
tagline: Guidance on procedures in Santa Cruz de la Sierra
summary: A public pilot that answers from published municipal fact sheets. If a procedure is not in the catalog, it says so and may search the web. It is not a City Hall site.
problem: Municipal procedure information in Santa Cruz de la Sierra is scattered across sources that are hard for a citizen to assemble into clear guidance without inventing requirements.
whyItMatters: People need orientation they can trust without treating an independent pilot as an official City Hall channel.
solution: A RAG assistant over published procedure fact sheets, with a Next.js interface and a FastAPI/LangGraph backend backed by PostgreSQL and pgvector.
approach: Retrieval is anchored to published procedure cards in the catalog. When a procedure is missing, the system states that explicitly and may fall back to a web search for orientation only. Editorial review and in-person verification remain outside the model and are not claimed as automated guarantees.
engineeringDecisions:
  - Answers are anchored to published fact sheets rather than free-form speculation about requirements.
  - Missing catalog coverage produces an explicit negative instead of inventing steps.
  - Relevance and source checks gate what is presented as grounded in the catalog.
  - The public pilot is framed as independent orientation, not as the City Hall site.
validation: Focused retrieval and source-check exercises described in the portfolio materials confirm that answers stay tied to published sheets and that gaps are acknowledged; no accuracy percentage is claimed.
benefits:
  - Offers public orientation for Santa Cruz de la Sierra municipal procedures from a published catalog.
  - Separates catalog-grounded answers from optional internet fallback.
  - Keeps the pilot clearly distinct from official City Hall channels.
limitations: Scope is a pilot; availability may vary. The site is not official City Hall. Internet fallback is orientation only and is not a municipal source of truth.
